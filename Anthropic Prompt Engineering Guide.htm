Letting Claude think through complex tasks like research, analysis, or problem-solving with the "chain of thought" (CoT) prompting technique can significantly improve its performance. This method encourages Claude to break down problems step-by-step, leading to more accurate and nuanced outputs. There are different ways to prompt for thinking, ordered from least to most complex. Basic prompts include a simple instruction like "Think step-by-step," which lacks guidance on how to think. Guided prompts outline specific steps for Claude to follow in its thinking process, while structured prompts use XML tags to separate reasoning from the final answer. To get the best out of CoT, it's essential to understand your success criteria and have a clear definition of what you want to achieve. You should also be able to empirically test against those criteria and have a first draft prompt that needs improvement. This guide focuses on success criteria that can be controlled through prompt engineering. It suggests trying different techniques in order, starting with the most broadly effective methods.
